Intelligence is usually treated as a competence an agent has, a capacity for solving problems toward a goal. Intelligence is Immanent inverts that order. Drawing on Simondon's individuation, Weinbaum's open-ended intelligence, Peirce's interpretants, and Deleuze's plane of immanence, it argues that intelligence is the formative process by which thinking agents are constituted at all — knowledge individuating through encounter rather than cargo transmitted intact. The essay builds a process model in four movements — ingression, individuation, circulation, and egression — and asks what organizational forms (institutions, substrates, extitutions) sustain the conditions of further becoming, and which failure modes (over-convergence, over-variety, enclosure, circulation failures) terminate it. Intelligence belongs to no single mind; it is the spiraling itself.
